North America Spending Explorer

Where households spend more or less than average, by state and province, and how that is changing.

In plain English

This dashboard shows how households in US states and Canadian provinces split their spending across eight broad categories, and how that has shifted since 2011. Use it to spot categories that are growing, or where a region spends noticeably more or less than its neighbours.

How to read these numbers
  • Share of budget: out of every $100 a typical household spends, how many dollars go to this category.
  • Index: compares a region's share with the average region in the same country. 100 is average, 120 means 20% more than average, 80 means 20% less.
  • 5-year change: how far the share moved over about five years, in percentage points. The Canadian survey skips some years, so the nearest available year is used.
  • Real spending growth: the yearly change in dollars after removing inflation, per person in the US and per household in Canada.
  • Colours: blue means above average or rising, red means below average or falling, grey means no data.
  • Keep in mind: official figures are annual and arrive a year or two late. US and Canadian figures are measured differently, so compare within a country. This shows where money goes, not which product to make, so pair it with search trends and competitor research.
